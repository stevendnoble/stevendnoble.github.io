ingredients:
  - 3 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  - 5 cloves garlic, thinly sliced or minced
  - ¼ tsp red pepper flakes, optional
  - 2 cans whole peeled tomatoes, 28 oz each
  - 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  - ½ tsp black pepper
  - 1 tsp sugar, optional
  - 1 small parmesan rind, optional
  - ¼ cup fresh basil, torn
  - 1 tbsp butter, optional
instructions:
  - step:
    title: Start the garlic
    description: Heat olive oil in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Add garlic and red pepper flakes, if using, and cook for 1-2 minutes until fragrant but not browned.
  - step:
    title: Add tomatoes
    description: Crush the tomatoes by hand or with a spoon, then add them with their juices. Stir in salt, pepper, sugar if the tomatoes taste sharp, and parmesan rind if using.
  - step:
    title: Simmer
    description: Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 30-40 minutes, stirring occasionally, until slightly thickened.
  - step:
    title: Adjust texture
    description: Leave chunky, mash with a spoon, or blend briefly for a smoother sauce.
  - step:
    title: Finish
    description: Remove the parmesan rind. Stir in basil and butter if using, then taste and adjust salt.

# Marinara Sauce

## Simple Tomato Sauce

Marinara is one of those recipes where simple ingredients do most of the
work. Good canned tomatoes, garlic, olive oil, salt, and enough simmering
time make a sauce that can carry pasta, gnocchi, meatballs, pizza, or
sandwiches.

## Tips and Variations

- Use whole peeled tomatoes if you like a looser, more rustic sauce.
- Add red pepper flakes for heat or basil at the end for freshness.
- Simmer longer for a thicker sauce.
- Freeze extra sauce in small containers so it is easy to use later.
